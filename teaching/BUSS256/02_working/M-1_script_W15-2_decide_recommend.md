---

**[1] Open — zoom out**
- "Two weeks ago, text was just... text. Now you can take 575 reviews and turn them into a decision. Today we close the loop: which tool do you actually use, what do you tell the hotel, and how much do you trust it. This is also our review, so if anything's been fuzzy, today's the day to un-fuzz it."

**[2] The big question: when do you even need the AI? [click]**
- "Honest moment. Sometimes the fancy AI is overkill. Think about it — 'what are these reviews mostly about?' Did we need an AI for that?"
- [pause; expect: "no — we just counted words"]
- "Right! Day one, no AI, we counted words and instantly saw: it's about room size and cleanliness. Reaching for an AI there would've added cost and risk for *nothing*. Use the cheapest tool that answers the question."

**[3] So when does the AI earn its keep? [click]**
- "The AI earned its keep on the hard stuff — 'not ready', 'clean but tired', the sarcasm, the mixed reviews the word-counter was blind to. *That's* where it's worth it. But — and you can finish this sentence for me now —"
- [expect the room: "only after you check it!"] → "Only after you check it. Three weeks ago that sentence would've sounded paranoid. Now it sounds like common sense. That's growth."
- "So the rule you leave with: cheapest tool that works; reach for the AI only when the nuance demands it; and never, ever ship an answer you haven't checked against your ruler or your gold standard."

**[4] From numbers to a recommendation [click]**
- "Now the part that makes you useful in a job. A sentiment score isn't a deliverable. A *decision* is. So what do we actually tell this hotel?"
- "Across everything — the word counts, the TF-IDF, the reviews you read — the same message keeps coming up: guests *love* that it's clean, and they *complain* that the rooms feel small. So the real problem isn't cleanliness — they're nailing that. It's space."
- "And notice — I'm basing that on the *transparent* evidence, the word counts we can all check. Remember the AI's category-sorting was kind of a mess last class? So we *don't* lean on it for the headline. We lean on the part we verified. A good recommendation sounds like: 'Guests consistently praise cleanliness but feel rooms are cramped — so invest in storage and smart layout, and set honest size expectations when people book, instead of spending more on cleaning you're already great at.' Problem, evidence, action. Done."

**[5] Always say the limits out loud [click]**
- "One thing that separates a pro from an amateur: a pro states the limits. Two of them, in plain words."
- "*Did we measure the right thing?* — we checked that by hand-labeling and comparing. That's validity. *Is the answer stable?* — we checked that by running it more than once, cache off. That's reliability. You report both, and you flag the close calls for a human. Confidence is not the same as being right — your *checking* is what makes it right."

**[6] The whole course, in one module [click — the thesis slide]**
- "Step way back. Remember day one of this whole course — we said that when machines automate the work, your value moves from *doing the task* to *judging and verifying* it. People thought that was just a nice slogan. This module is the proof."
- "The AI read 575 reviews in seconds. It did the *labor*. But every decision that mattered — what to ask, which categories, whether to believe it, what to tell the hotel — that was *you*. And you could only make those calls because you held a ruler you understood. The machine executes. You direct, you verify, you decide. That's the job. That's what this whole degree is quietly preparing you for."

**[7] Quick review — lock the keep-card [interactive]**
- "Rapid fire, shout the answer. 'What are these about?' —" [expect: "count words / TF-IDF, no AI"]
- "'Happy or unhappy, with the tricky context?' —" [expect: "AI sentiment — then verify"]
- "'Sort into my categories?' —" [expect: "AI classify — check against a gold standard"]
- "'How do I trust any of it?' —" [expect: "hand-first, then verify; report validity and reliability"]
- "That's the card. If you remember nothing else, remember that order."

**[8] Beginner reassurance + close**
- [if someone says "I still don't feel like a coder"] → "You don't have to be. You ran real analyses, caught an AI's mistakes, and made a business call from data. That's data literacy. The syntax you can always look up; the judgment is the rare part, and you've got it."
- "So — text mining: count to see what's there, let the AI read the hard stuff, and *always* check it against something you understand. Machines execute; you decide. That's the module, that's the course, and honestly, that's the skill that'll outlast whatever model is popular next year. Nicely done, all of you."
