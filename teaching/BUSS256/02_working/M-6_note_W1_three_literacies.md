## Where this sits
You've just seen the **two literacies** this course builds:
- **Data literacy** — reading data: where it comes from, what it measures, whether it's any good, what it can
  and can't tell you.
- **Algorithm literacy** — reading methods: what a technique (a regression, a clustering, a chart) actually
  does, and when it's the right tool.

These are the new reading and writing of a data-driven world. This course is mostly about earning them **by
hand** — you'll compute and interpret things yourself, because that's how you come to *trust* them.

## The third literacy: working with AI
There's a **third** skill arriving in your working life, and we'll name it now even though we won't use any AI
tool until after the midterm:

> **Working with AI** — being able to **direct** an AI to do analytical work, and, just as important, **verify**
> whether what it gave you is actually right.

Here's the honest version of why this matters. AI tools are **fast**, they sound **fluent and confident**, and
they are **sometimes confidently wrong** — they'll hand you a clean, professional-looking answer that is subtly
(or completely) incorrect. And here's the catch: **you can only catch that if you already know what right looks
like.** That's why this course teaches the hand skills *first*. The two literacies aren't being replaced by AI —
they're what **lets you supervise** it.

So the three fit together simply:
- **Data literacy** + **algorithm literacy** = you know what a right answer looks like.
- **Working with AI** = you can get an answer fast **and tell whether it's right.**
Without the first two, the third is just trusting a confident stranger.

## What this means for the next few weeks (no AI yet)
For now — Weeks 1 through 5 — there is **no AI in this course.** You'll learn R, data structures, and how to
manage and describe data, all by hand. But you'll notice a small recurring habit in the exercises: *"before you
trust the screen, check the answer yourself."* Predict a count, eyeball a mean, sanity-check a filter. That
habit is the seed of the third literacy. You're learning to **hold a standard** — so that later, when an AI
hands you an answer, you have something to hold it up against.

## Forward-pointer (one line)
> **After the midterm, we'll start working with AI — and the rule will be the same every time: hold the
> standard, direct the AI, verify it against your standard.** We'll learn it for real in the visualization week
> (Week 7). Today, just remember the name: it's the **third literacy**, and you build it on the back of the
> other two.

## Key takeaways
- This course builds **three** literacies: **data**, **algorithm**, and — newly named — **working with AI**.
- Working with AI = **direct it** *and* **verify it**; verifying only works if you already know what right
  looks like, which is why **hand skills come first.**
- AI is fast and fluent but **sometimes confidently wrong** — your own judgment is the safeguard, not the
  bottleneck.
- No AI tools until after the midterm; for now you're building the standard you'll later hold AI to.
