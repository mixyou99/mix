# Spoken Script — W1 framing: The Third Literacy

*Change log: Jun 18, 2026 — created (Stage 2). Spoken companion to `M-6_note_W1_three_literacies.md`. The only
new spoken content in M-6 (§8.6 — the rest of the first half keeps its existing scripts). Spoken register per
STYLE_STANDARDS §5: locked playful/warm tone, casual, English. **Conceptual only — no tool, no live demo.**
Pitched to Week-1 students (no R yet). `[brackets]` = stage directions, not spoken.*

> Slots in right after the existing "two literacies" beat in Seminar 1. ~2–3 minutes. Keep it light — you're
> planting a flag, not teaching a method.

---

**[1] Pick up from the two literacies**
- "Okay — so I just told you this whole course is about building two literacies. **Data literacy** — you can
  read data: where's it from, is it any good, what can it actually tell me. And **algorithm literacy** — you can
  read the *methods*: what's a regression really doing, is this even the right tool. The new reading and writing.
  Cool. Now I want to add a **third** one — and then we're not going to touch it again for two months."

**[2] Name the third literacy**
- "The third one is: **working with AI.** And I mean two things by that — being able to **tell an AI what
  analytical work to do**, *and* — this is the real one — being able to **tell whether what it handed back is
  actually right.**"
- [lean in] "Because here's the thing nobody says out loud. These AI tools? **Fast. Fluent. Super confident.**
  And **sometimes confidently, beautifully wrong.** It'll give you a gorgeous, professional-looking answer in
  two seconds — charts, numbers, the works — and it's just... off. Subtly. Sometimes completely."

**[3] The catch — why hand skills come first**
- "And catch *this*, because it's the whole reason this course is built the way it is: **you can only spot a
  wrong answer if you already know what a right one looks like.** That's it. That's the punchline."
- "So I'm going to make you do everything **by hand** first. Compute it yourself, interpret it yourself, sweat it
  a little. Not because I'm cruel — [beat] okay, not *only* because I'm cruel — but because that's how you earn
  the thing that lets you **supervise** an AI instead of just... believing it."
- "Two literacies plus knowing what right looks like — that's what turns 'I asked the AI' into 'I asked the AI
  **and I checked.**' Without the first two, that third skill is just **trusting a confident stranger.** Don't be
  the person who trusts the confident stranger."

**[4] What happens for now**
- "So here's the deal for the next five weeks: **no AI.** None. We do R, we do data, all by hand. But you're
  going to notice a tiny habit sneaking into the exercises — every so often I'll say *'before you trust the
  screen, check it yourself.'* Guess the count, eyeball the average, sanity-check your filter. That little habit?
  That's the seed. You're quietly learning to **hold a standard.**"

**[5] Forward-pointer + land it**
- "And then after the midterm, we start working with AI for real — first time's the visualization week, Week 7 —
  and the rule will never change: **hold the standard, direct the AI, verify it against your standard.** Hold,
  direct, verify. You'll hear me say it a hundred times."
- "For today, just remember the name. **Three** literacies now: data, algorithm, and **working with AI.** And
  that third one stands on the shoulders of the other two. Alright — let's go learn what data even *is*."
