# STYLE_STANDARDS

How generated materials should sound and be formatted. Derived from Prof. Lee's
Seminar 1 script and slides. Apply to all Big Data Analytics materials; adapt
altitude (not voice) for MBA and external versions.

---

## 1. Voice & tone

- **Warm, personal, mentor-like.** The professor frames himself as a guide
  ("like your friend or uncle, not just a teacher"), not a distant lecturer.
- **Conversational and direct.** Short spoken sentences, frequent rhetorical
  questions to pull students in ("Can anyone tell me what an algorithm is?").
- **Light, self-aware humor.** Used sparingly to lower the temperature (e.g.,
  the running joke about summer break being too short; acknowledging his own
  "Konglish" accent). Keep it gentle and inclusive.
- **Encouraging toward beginners.** Repeatedly reassures students with no coding
  background that they will succeed. Never condescending.
- **Career-relevant framing.** Ties concepts to hiring demand and future
  leadership ("HR managers want people who can communicate with data").

## 2. Accessibility

- English written for a mixed, partly non-native audience: plain vocabulary,
  one idea per sentence, define terms on first use.
- Invite clarification explicitly; avoid idioms that don't translate.

## 3. Recurring rhetorical moves (reuse these)

- **Then-vs-now contrast** to motivate a topic (language literacy a decade ago →
  data/algorithm literacy now).
- **Concrete familiar examples first**, abstraction second (YouTube/Netflix
  recommendation algorithms before defining "algorithm").
- **Real datasets and named companies** for credibility (App Store, Airbnb,
  Facebook, Netflix, Amazon).
- **"Why this matters to you"** close on each major concept.

## 4. Slide conventions (from Seminar 1 deck)

- Title + concise bullets; one concept per slide; numbered slides.
- Opening student survey to read the room; "Today's Objectives" early; "What's
  Next…" closing slide with reading + assignment for the next session.
- Source line on borrowed definitions/stats (e.g., McKinsey 2024; cite year).
- Visuals and short video clips are welcomed ("generous use of multimedia").

## 5. Lecture-script conventions (from Script1)

- Spoken register — write what is *said*, not prose to be read silently.
- Bracketed section markers as beats: `[1] Greeting`, `[2] Survey`, etc.,
  matching slide flow.
- Bullet-per-utterance pacing; include interaction cues and expected
  student responses inline.
- Build in pauses, check-ins ("Do you understand what you have to do? Yes?
  Okay."), and transitions between slides.
- **Spoken-script tone for this course:** high humor and warmth, casual/playful,
  beginner-friendly, direct address ("you") — see the M-1 scripts as the reference
  (e.g., "Listen to you"; light sarcasm like "Thanks, very helpful"; "Nicely done,
  all of you"). Keep casual **accurate** — never sacrifice correctness for ease.
- **Delivery language: English.** Scripts are near-verbatim teaching text (what the
  professor says), not just an outline. Applies to all module scripts (M-2…M-6).

## 6. Lecture-note conventions

- Bridge between slide (sparse) and script (spoken): full-sentence explanation a
  student can study from, with the worked examples spelled out.
- Each note: learning objectives → concept → worked example (with real dataset)
  → "when/how to use" → common mistakes / how to verify → key takeaways.

## 7. Terminology — keep consistent

- "Business Analytics (BA)"; "Data literacy (DL)"; "Algorithm literacy."
- "data-analytic thinking"; "data-driven decision making."
- Course techniques named exactly: association rules, regression, clustering,
  text-mining, data visualization.
- When introducing AI tools, name them plainly (Claude, ChatGPT) and keep
  model-version specifics in `_currency/` so the stable notes don't date.

## 8. Formatting hygiene

- Avoid over-formatting. Use headers and bullets only where they aid clarity;
  default to clean prose in notes.
- Consistent date format (Mon DD, YYYY) and consistent spelling (US English, to
  match existing materials).
- Every revised file carries a one-line change log at top (date + change).
- Printed or photocopied deliverables follow the black-and-white production
  standard: `_shared_library/production/BW_PRODUCTION_STANDARD.md` (meaning must
  never depend on color).
